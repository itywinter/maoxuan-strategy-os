# Dominant Aspect Analysis

## Purpose

After a primary contradiction is sufficiently identified, determine which side currently exerts greater causal/constraint power.

Dominant does not mean morally wrong, incompetent, or solely responsible.

## Two-sided intervention test

- Improve Side A substantially while B stays roughly unchanged. What happens?
- Improve Side B substantially while A stays roughly unchanged. What happens?

The side whose unchanged condition continues to constrain the system more strongly is the leading dominant aspect.

Assess causal strength, necessity, hard-constraint power, sensitivity, persistence, stage relevance, and evidence quality.

## Status

Use: **confirmed, leading, competing, coupled, not ready**.

If both sides independently impose hard constraints, do not force a winner; design a test that distinguishes them.

## Anti-comfort rule

Do not choose the explanation that is psychologically easier. External constraints may dominate; internal shortcomings may dominate. Evidence decides.
